The Earth layer is generated by `tools/data/build_world.py` from these sources. Run `python3 tools/data/fetch.py` and then `python3 tools/data/build_world.py` to rebuild it. The generator needs numpy and Pillow.

## Choices the generator makes

- **Nations:** every sovereign country in Natural Earth's 1:50m set, except Antarctica.
  - Dependencies belong to their sovereign, e.g. Greenland to Denmark and Puerto Rico to the United States.
  - European microstates are folded into the surrounding country. These are Vatican City and San Marino (Italy), Monaco (France), Andorra (Spain) and Liechtenstein (Switzerland).
  - Island microstates form two groups, as Terra Invicta does for small neighbours: the Eastern Caribbean States and the Pacific Island States.
  - Disputed areas follow de facto administration: Somaliland → Somalia, Northern Cyprus → Cyprus, Western Sahara → Morocco, Siachen → India. Kosovo, Palestine and Taiwan are their own nations.
- **Regions:** each nation's provinces are clustered into regions with population-weighted k-means. The number of regions grows with population and land area, for about 360 in total. A region is named after its most populous province.
- **Breakaway homelands** (`tools/data/breakaways.json`, from `build_breakaways.py`): each of Terra Invicta's breakaway nations gets one more region in its parent, so it can secede without taking the capital. Its capital's province (snapped from Terra Invicta's rough map coordinates, the one nearest its centre winning a clash) and the parent's capital province are pinned as cluster centres, and the k-means fills in around them. That gives 478 regions. A breakaway's region is named after its capital city (Glasgow, Barcelona, Dallas), or after its province if another region of the nation already has that name. Some regions are named by hand in `build_world.py`: breakaway homelands in `REGION_NAMES` (North West Territories, St. John's) and other regions in `REGION_RENAMES` (Canada's provinces by their capitals: Victoria, Regina, Winnipeg, Ottawa, Halifax). Canada's capital is pinned to Ontario, since Ottawa's point falls in Québec on our raster.
- **Region population** splits the national total by the population of the region's cities, plus a small area term.

## Claims — `tools/data/build_claims.py`

Run order: `build_breakaways.py`, `build_world.py`, `build_claims.py`, then `build_research.py`. Changing the regions means old saves can't be loaded (`src/sim/save.ts`).

Claims come from Terra Invicta's TIBilateralTemplate (relationType "Claim"), with TIRegionTemplate, TIMapRegionTemplate (region latitude and longitude) and TINationTemplate to match its map to ours (`src/content/claims.json`). Run it before `build_research.py`, which keeps the projects that unlock claims. How the matching works is in `docs/formulas.md` ("Claims").

## Solar System — `tools/data/build_solar.py`

The bodies and their orbits come from Terra Invicta's TISpaceBodyTemplate, in the game's install or the same `Pzixel/terra-invicta-techtree-update` repository; the script's docstring has the command. It keeps every body with a full set of orbital elements (485 bodies): the Sun, the planets, their moons, the 125 asteroidal moons (Terra Invicta's class for small irregular moons such as Phobos, Deimos and most of the giants' moons, plus the small moons of Pluto, Haumea and Quaoar), the dwarf planets, asteroids and comets, with names, radii, masses and Keplerian elements. Moons' semi-major axes are converted from km to AU; for Jupiter's moons the argument of periapsis and mean anomaly are worked out from the longitude of periapsis and mean longitude the game gives. Fourteen bodies whose elements the game leaves incomplete are filled in by `tools/data/solar_overrides.json` (each entry names its source): the comets Pons-Winnecke and Lovas 2 and eight distant objects from the JPL Small-Body Database (retrieved 2026-10-29), Styx and Kerberos from JPL's Pluto satellite elements (Brozović & Jacobson 2024), Hiʻiaka from Ragozzine & Brown 2009 (the fit the game's Namaka comes from) and Weywot from Proudfoot et al. 2025. Masses the game lacks for them are estimates from its diameters, at 1.0 g/cm³ for trans-Neptunian objects and 0.5 for comet nuclei, except Gǃkúnǁʼhòmdímà's (measured, Grundy et al. 2019). The game's "512381" is a typo for (528381) 2008 ST291, whose orbit and size it gives. Left out: the hypothetical Planet Nine and the 9 bodies whose orbits are not yet published in full (Dysnomia, Vanth, Ilmarë, Actaea, Gǃòʼé ǃHú, Xiangliu, Tinia, Linus and Menoetius). Some dwarf-planet names are Terra Invicta's own for unnamed objects.

## Surface maps of other bodies — `tools/data/build_body_maps.py`

Thirty maps in `public/map` (each 1024 × 512, equirectangular, −180° to 180° east like `earth.png`): Mercury, Venus, Luna, Mars, Ceres, Jupiter and its four great moons, Saturn and seven of its moons, Uranus and its five great moons, Neptune, Triton, Pluto, Eris, Haumea and Makemake. They are built by `python3 -I tools/data/build_body_maps.py [body …]` from public-domain sources that `fetch.py` downloads (about 4.2 GB in all):

Mercury's and the Moon's mosaics are photometrically flat, so their relief is a hillshade of the DEM lit from the north-west, mixed into the brightness; Mars's Viking mosaic is made of sunlit images and gets a little of MOLA's on top; Ceres's has only its own. Colours are pulled back from the mosaics' stretch to each body's real tones, keeping the regional differences (Mercury's bluish low-reflectance material and warmer smooth plains, the Moon's bluish maria). Gaps are filled from around them; where a mosaic is poor near a pole (Mercury north of 78° and south of 72°S, Ceres beyond 80°) brightness and colour fade to the average, and Mars's messy caps become clean ice beyond 81°N and 84°S. Jupiter is warmed a little towards Cassini's and Juno's true-colour views, Ganymede's dark terrain made browner; Europa's and Callisto's greyscale mosaics are coloured along a ramp of their real tones (Europa's white-tan ice and reddish-brown lineae, Callisto's dark brown-grey and bright ejecta), their poorly imaged poles fading to the average. The Galilean moons' mosaics give longitudes positive west, −180° to 180°, but are drawn east to the right as ours are, so they need no turning (Loki lands at 13°N, 51°E as it should). Saturn's moons' greyscale mosaics are coloured along ramps of their tones the same way (Enceladus almost pure white, Iapetus's leading face dark reddish-brown), their smeared poles fading to the average beyond 80°. Their labels disagree on centre longitudes, but read as ISIS lays them out (east to the right, x from the projection's centre longitude), only Titan's (0°–360°) needs turning half a turn; checked by Xanadu (100°W) being Titan's brightest equatorial region, Herschel on Mimas's leading face at 112°W and Odysseus on Tethys near 33°N, 129°W. Titan's surface only shades its pale orange haze a little, as in visible light. Voyager 2 flew past Uranus in 1986 with the system's south pole towards the Sun, so its moons were imaged only south of about their equators (USGS's mosaics on `planetarymaps.usgs.gov` hold none of them; JPL's copies do). The maps run east to the right from −180° as ours do, checked by Ursula on Titania (12°S, 45°E), Hamlet on Oberon (46°S, 44°E), Wunda on Umbriel (8°S, 86°W) and Miranda's Arden and Elsinore coronae. The rest of each moon is made up from its own imaged ground: 48-pixel patches of the well-imaged band from 62°S to 18°S, their broad tone taken out, picked at random with a fixed seed and laid overlapping under raised-cosine windows (laid on polar maps beyond 62°, so the poles don't pinch), on the moon's average tone; the shadowed edge of the coverage is trimmed and feathered in, and the mosaics' broad swings of brightness halved. Then they are coloured along ramps of the moons' tones (Ariel's bright bluish grey, Umbriel's sooty dark with Wunda's bright ring, Titania's and Oberon's faintly warm). In the game's years the Sun stands high over their northern hemispheres (Uranus's northern solstice is in 2028), so the sunlit faces are mostly the made-up ones. Triton's and Pluto's unseen parts are made up the same way (`unseen()`), but start from the tone along the edge of the coverage rather than the average: Triton's far north from its ground between 35°S and 25°N (its brightness on a ramp from grey to pale peach-pink, with half the mosaic's own colour, Voyager's green cast taken out), Pluto's south beyond about 30°S, in polar night when New Horizons came by in 2015, from its ground between 20°S and 55°N. Pluto's map runs 0°–360° east and is turned half a turn (Sputnik Planitia lands at 175°E, Cthulhu to its west); its greyscale goes on a ramp of its MVIC colours, tholin-dark red-brown through tan-orange to the cream of Sputnik's nitrogen ice, the north polar Lowell Regio a little greyer. Eris (almost white methane frost, albedo 0.96), Haumea (faintly bluish crystalline water ice with Lacerda et al.'s darker, redder spot on the long axis at 0°) and Makemake (bright ice tinged reddish-tan, patchy) are value noise sampled on the sphere, so they neither pinch at the poles nor seam. Then each goes onto a palette with ordered dithering, as for the Earth: hand-picked, except for Jupiter, Io and Ganymede, whose many hues get a palette fitted to the map by k-means.

Saturn's rings are drawn in `bodyArt.ts` at their real radii (C ring, B ring, Cassini Division, A ring with the Encke Gap) in the plane square to Saturn's pole, with Saturn's shadow on them and theirs on Saturn; Uranus's narrow, nearly black rings the same way, faintly (rings 6–4, α–β and η–δ as three soft bands, then the ε ring, widened a little to show as pixels). Neptune's rings the same way, fainter still (Galle, Le Verrier, Lassell out to Arago, and Adams without its arcs), and Haumea's single ring, 70 km wide at 2,287 km and half opaque (Ortiz et al. 2017). Haumea is drawn as the ellipsoid it is, 1,161 × 852 × 513 km, ray-cast in its own frame and lit by its true normals. Neptune's map is bluer than Uranus's, with faint bands, a dark collar near 60°–70°S, a dark spot at 22°N (as Hubble saw in 2018) with bright methane clouds on its poleward edge, white streaks at 20°–30°S and 25°–45°N, and the small Scooter at 42°S. Uranus's map is a pale cyan globe with faint bands, a whitish north polar cap edged by a darker lane near 60°N, a fainter collar round the south pole as Voyager saw it, and a few small bright clouds at 25°–50°N. Venus's map is a cream-white globe with a soft, mottled peach haze across the low latitudes, white wisps sweeping poleward in chevrons, a faint greyer collar and bright polar hoods, made from fixed-seed value noise.

The bodies turn to the IAU's rotational elements (Archinal et al. 2018; `src/app/solar/bodyFrames.ts`), so the maps' longitudes face the right way: Mercury, Mars (the 2009 elements, periodic terms left out) and Ceres (Dawn's). The Moon and the great moons of Jupiter, Saturn and Uranus keep their near sides towards their planets as they go round. Venus turns with its cloud tops, westward in about four days, not its surface; Jupiter and Saturn at System III, their magnetic fields' rates (their clouds have no fixed longitudes); Uranus at Voyager's radio period, backwards, as its IAU north pole is the one on the Solar System's north side; Neptune at its interior's rate, its pole nodding a little. Triton faces Neptune, its pole swinging by up to 32° with its precessing orbit (the IAU's periodic terms, kept). Pluto turns to the IAU's elements (longitude 0° facing Charon). Eris is locked to Dysnomia, its pole Dysnomia's orbit pole (RA 36.17°, Dec 44.51°; Holler et al. 2021) and its day 15.79 days; Haumea's pole is its ring's and Hi'iaka's orbit's (RA 285.1°, Dec −10.6°) and its day 3.92 hours; Makemake's day is 22.83 hours (Hromakina et al. 2019), and its unknown pole is put square to our line of sight (its moon's orbit is seen edge-on) as near the ecliptic's pole as that allows (RA 325.9°, Dec 56.2°). Their prime meridians are unknown and set at 0° at J2000.

## Research — `tools/data/build_research.py`

Techs and projects come from Terra Invicta's own template files and English localization: TITechTemplate, TIProjectTemplate and TIEffectTemplate. These are published with the community tech tree site ([pzixel.github.io/terra-invicta-techtree-update](https://pzixel.github.io/terra-invicta-techtree-update), repository `Pzixel/terra-invicta-techtree-update`, folder `public/gamefiles/stable`). The script's docstring has the commands.

- **Techs** (`src/content/techs.json`): names, categories, research costs, prerequisites, summaries, and the effects our simulation applies.
- **Projects** (`src/content/projects.json`): names (faction names swapped for ours), categories, costs, prerequisites, faction restrictions, unlock and availability chances, summaries, one-time grants and effects. Effects are translated into our own effect kinds. Which projects are kept is in `docs/formulas.md` ("Research").
